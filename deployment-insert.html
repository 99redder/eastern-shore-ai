Survival Node

How to Deploy Your Kit

Step-by-step instructions for getting your Survival Node up and running when you need it — written for anyone, no tech experience required.

Keep This Document With Your Case

Do not throw this guide away — keep it with your Survival Node case for easy reference when deploying your kit.

Phase 1 — Open the Case and Get the Phone

1

Unlock and open the hard case

If you used the padlock, use your key to remove it. Flip the two latches on the front of the case outward, then lift the lid. The case opens like a suitcase.

2

Retrieve the phone from its Faraday bag

The phone is stored inside a dark, metallic-looking pouch — this is the Faraday bag. It may be folded over and sealed at the top. Unfold the top of the bag, open it, and slide the phone out.

The Faraday bag is what protects the phone from electromagnetic pulses (EMPs). You only open it when you're ready to use the phone.

3

Retrieve the solar battery from its Faraday bag

The solar battery is also stored in a Faraday bag. Open it the same way and remove the battery. Set it nearby — you'll use it in Phase 2.

Phase 2 — Power On the Phone

4

Press and hold the power button

The power button is on the right side of the phone. Press and hold it for 2–3 seconds until you feel a vibration and see the screen light up. Release the button and wait for the phone to finish starting up — this usually takes about 30–60 seconds.

If the phone does not turn on, the battery may be too low. Skip to Step 6 to charge it first, then come back and power it on.

5

Turn on Airplane Mode immediately

Once the phone is on, turn on Airplane Mode right away. This stops the phone from constantly searching for a cell tower signal, which drains the battery very fast — even when no signal is available.

How to turn on Airplane Mode:

  1. Swipe down from the very top of the screen to pull down the notification bar.
  2. Look for an icon that looks like an airplane. Tap it once. It will turn blue or highlighted when active.
  3. That's it — Airplane Mode is on. All offline features (AI, maps, reference libraries) still work normally.
Important: Keep Airplane Mode ON whenever you are not actively making a phone call or sending a text. It is the single most important thing you can do to preserve battery life.
6

Lower the screen brightness

A bright screen is one of the biggest battery drains. Lower it as much as you comfortably can.

How to lower brightness:

  1. Swipe down from the top of the screen.
  2. You will see a slider (usually a sun icon with a horizontal bar). Slide it to the left to lower brightness.
  3. Aim for the lowest level you can comfortably read.

Phase 3 — Check Battery Levels and Connect Solar Power

7

Check the phone's battery level

Look at the top-right corner of the phone screen. You'll see a small battery icon showing how much charge is remaining. A percentage number may also be visible.

  • 50% or above — you have plenty of power. Proceed normally.
  • 20–49% — connect to the solar battery soon to keep the level up.
  • Below 20% — connect to the solar battery now before doing anything else.
8

Check the solar battery's charge level

Press the power button on the solar battery (it's a small button, usually on the side or top). Four small blue LED lights will briefly appear, showing how much charge remains:

  • 4 LEDs lit — fully charged, ready for extended use
  • 3 LEDs lit — good charge remaining
  • 2 LEDs lit — moderate charge, use conservatively
  • 1 LED lit — low, prioritize charging over the phone first when possible
  • 0 LEDs / no lights — critically low; place solar panel in direct sunlight immediately
9

Connect the phone to the solar battery

The solar battery has cables permanently attached to it. Find the USB-C cable (it's the small oval-shaped connector — not the flat one, not the round one). Plug the USB-C end into the charging port on the bottom of the phone.

The phone will begin charging automatically. You do not need to press anything. You can use the phone while it charges.

The charging port on the phone is at the very bottom center of the device. If the cable doesn't fit easily, flip it over — USB-C connectors work both ways.

10

Place the solar panel in direct sunlight

The solar battery has a dark panel on its back side. This panel converts sunlight into power that slowly recharges the battery. To get the most out of it:

  • Place the solar battery with the panel face-up in direct overhead sunlight — not shade, not indirect light.
  • Angle it toward the sun and re-adjust as the sun moves throughout the day.
  • Do not place it on hot surfaces such as asphalt, metal, or car hoods — surface heat compounds battery heat and can cause damage.
  • Solar charging is slow. It is not meant to replace fast charging — it is meant to keep your battery from running out during extended emergencies.
⚠ Hot weather safety — check on it regularly. In warm or hot weather, a lithium battery charging in direct sunlight can overheat. Check the solar battery every 20–30 minutes by picking it up and feeling it. It should feel warm, not hot. If it feels uncomfortably hot to the touch, move it to a shaded area and let it cool down before resuming solar charging. Never leave it unattended for long periods in extreme heat. Do not charge above 113°F (45°C).
Do not connect the solar battery's input charger port and an output cable at the same time. Only charge the solar battery from a wall outlet OR use it to charge the phone — not both simultaneously.

Phase 4 — Using the Phone

Everything on your Survival Node works without internet, Wi-Fi, or cell signal. The apps are already installed and ready. Here is how to use the most important ones.

Ask the AI a Question (PocketPal)

PocketPal is the AI chat app on your phone. Your Survival Node comes with a pre-configured AI assistant called "Survival Node" that is tuned specifically for survival, medical questions, and emergency response.

  1. Find the PocketPal app on the home screen (it looks like a chat bubble icon) and tap it to open.
  2. At the top of the screen, tap the name of the currently selected AI model — it will show a dropdown. Select "Survival Node" if it is not already selected.
  3. Type your question in the box at the bottom of the screen and tap the send button (arrow icon). Or tap the microphone icon to speak your question out loud.
  4. Wait for the AI to respond — it may take 10–30 seconds depending on how complex the question is. This is normal.

What to ask it: first aid procedures, how to treat an injury, water purification methods, shelter building, navigation, equipment repair, emergency signals, food foraging — anything you would ask a knowledgeable person in an emergency.

Always verify critical information against a second source on the device (WikiMed, the Survival Manual app, etc.) before acting on it, especially for medical decisions. The AI is a powerful reference tool — not a replacement for professional help when it's available.

Navigate with Offline Maps (Organic Maps)

Your phone has the entire United States pre-loaded as offline maps. GPS navigation works even with no cell signal or internet.

  1. Find the Organic Maps app on the home screen (green map icon) and tap it to open.
  2. The map will show your current location as a blue dot. If you don't see it right away, wait 30–60 seconds for the phone to lock onto GPS satellites — this is normal, especially if you haven't used GPS recently.
  3. To navigate somewhere, tap the search bar at the top, type in a location or address, and tap the result. Then tap "Directions" and choose your travel mode (car, walking, etc.).
  4. Tap Start to begin turn-by-turn navigation.

GPS does not require internet or cell signal — it uses satellites. Airplane Mode does not affect GPS. Navigation will work even in a complete blackout.

Look Up Medical Information (Kiwix — WikiMed)

Your phone has the complete offline Wikipedia Medical Encyclopedia — thousands of articles covering symptoms, injuries, medications, and emergency care procedures.

  1. Find the Kiwix app on the home screen and tap it to open.
  2. You will see a list of offline libraries. Tap WikiMed Medical Encyclopedia.
  3. Use the search bar at the top to search for any medical topic — a condition, symptom, medication name, or procedure.
  4. Read the article. Medical articles include symptoms, causes, treatment options, and when to seek professional care.

Look Up Survival Skills (Survival Manual App)

The Survival Manual app is based on the US Army Field Manual and covers shelter, fire, water, food, first aid, and navigation — completely offline.

  1. Find the Survival Manual app on the home screen and tap it.
  2. Browse the categories (Shelter, Fire, Water, Food, First Aid, Navigation, etc.) or use search to find a specific topic.

Phase 5 — Managing Power During Extended Use

Your solar battery can keep the phone running for days or weeks under emergency use conditions. The key is discipline about how you use the phone.

11

The golden rule: use it, then shut it off

Every minute the screen is on drains the battery. In an emergency, you will use the phone for a specific task — look something up, check your location, ask the AI a question — and then power it completely off until you need it again.

How to power the phone off:

  1. Press and hold the power button on the right side of the phone for 2–3 seconds.
  2. A menu will appear on the screen with options like "Power off," "Restart," and "Emergency."
  3. Tap Power off and confirm if asked.
  4. The phone will shut down completely. This is what you want — a fully powered-off phone uses zero battery.

The solar battery recharges fastest when the phone is off and not drawing power. Brief, task-focused use followed by powering off is what allows solar alone to sustain the kit indefinitely.

12

If the phone battery gets very low

If the phone drops below 15%, connect it to the solar battery and keep the screen off (or turn the phone off entirely while charging) until it reaches at least 30–40% before using it again. Repeatedly draining a phone battery to 0% causes permanent damage.

13

If the solar battery gets very low

Place the solar battery panel-side up in the brightest direct sunlight available and leave it for as long as possible before connecting the phone. Solar charging is slow — give it several hours of direct sun to make meaningful progress.

Phase 6 — Communications (If Needed)

Off-grid messaging without a cell signal (Briar)

Briar is a messaging app pre-installed on your Survival Node that lets two people exchange text messages over a direct Bluetooth or Wi-Fi connection — no cell signal, no internet, no server required. Both people must have Briar installed and be within range of each other.

  1. Open Briar from the app drawer.
  2. If you have already added a contact, tap their name and send a message.
  3. If you need to add a new contact, both devices must be nearby — tap Add Contact and follow the on-screen instructions to exchange contact codes.

Emergency signaling (SOS Flashlight + Morse)

Your phone has two signaling tools for situations where audio or visual distress signals are needed:

  • SOS Flashlight — open the app and tap SOS. The phone's flashlight will automatically flash the international SOS signal (three short, three long, three short). This is recognized by emergency responders worldwide.
  • Morse — open the Morse app, type any message, and it will display or play the Morse code. Useful for signaling with a mirror, flashlight, or radio when voice communication is not possible.

Phase 7 — Putting It Away Between Uses

After each use session, follow these steps to reseal the kit properly so it is ready for the next time.

14

Power the phone completely off

Press and hold the power button, tap Power off, and confirm. Wait until the screen goes completely black and the phone is fully shut down before proceeding.

15

Place the phone back in its Faraday bag

Slide the phone into the Faraday bag. Fold the top of the bag over at least twice — fold it toward the back of the bag — and press the seal or roll it tight. A properly sealed Faraday bag should have no gaps or openings at the top.

The Faraday bag only protects the phone when it is fully sealed. A loose or partially open bag provides little to no protection against an EMP.

16

Place the solar battery back in its Faraday bag

Same process — slide it in, fold the top over at least twice, and seal it tightly.

17

Place both back in the hard case

Set the sealed Faraday bags and all other components back into the case. Close the lid firmly until both latches click into the locked position. Re-apply the padlock if you use one.
