How to use this page

You don't need to know when your kit was made. Each change starts with a quick "You already have this if…" check you can do on your phone. If you already have it, skip to the next one. If not, tap Show the steps.

Start with the Important ones. The Recommended ones add newer apps and libraries, and the Optional ones are about how the phone looks.

Questions about any step? Ask K, our AI assistant, or call or text us.

Every Survival Node is built on the same phone, a Motorola Moto G Power (2024), so every software change here works on every kit. Only the solar battery and Faraday bags changed over time: see Your battery and Faraday bags.

📱 Before you start

  • Do the steps on your Survival Node phone. To open this page on it, connect to Wi-Fi, open Fennec, tap the address bar and type:easternshore.ai/owners
  • Most steps need Wi-Fi with working internet, such as your home Wi-Fi. Use Wi-Fi you trust, not a coffee shop or hotel. Airplane mode can stay on; Wi-Fi works with it on. To turn Wi-Fi on, swipe down from the top of the screen twice and tap Wi-Fi.
  • Charge the phone to at least 50% first. Big downloads use battery.
  • When you're done, turn Wi-Fi off. Going back into storage? Charge the phone to about 60%, power it off and seal it in its Faraday bag.

Kits sold before about October 30, 2026 still have Google Play Services (turned off) and an older set of reference libraries. Every step on this page works on them too. If a Google Play Protect box appears when you install an app, tap More details, then Install anyway.

Important · Safety Applies to every kit · nothing to install

Look things up first: the AI can be dangerously wrong

Our ongoing testing across more survival topics found that both AI models on the phone sometimes give wrong, even dangerous, answers. It happens most on topics beyond drinking water and first aid, such as fallout, chemical spills, home canning, carbon monoxide and staying warm. Qwen 3.5 4B gets things wrong less often than Qwen 3.5 2B, but neither is reliable enough to act on by itself.

  • Look it up first in WikiMed, the Survival Manual or the other references on the phone. Use the AI to explain what you read or to suggest what to look up; it can't see the libraries itself.
  • Never act on an AI answer alone for medical care, drinking water, food, fire, carbon monoxide, radiation or chemicals. Follow emergency officials, and call 911 when you can.
  • If you do ask the AI, use Qwen 3.5 4B with Think off (see the Pal item below).

We're testing improved instructions that cover more topics, and they'll appear on this page when they're ready. Our Terms of Sale describe these limits under "AI Answers."

Update the Survival Node Pal's instructions

The Survival Node Pal in PocketPal is the AI set up for survival, first aid and field repair. On November 17, 2026 we rewrote the instructions behind it. In our ongoing, periodic QA testing, the earlier instructions led the AI to give unsafe drinking-water advice. With the new ones, the AI gives the standard, proven method first (the one the CDC, Red Cross, FEMA or U.S. Army teaches), doesn't make up numbers, and knows the CDC's drinking-water figures. On November 18 we tightened the bleach line to boiling first and one amount, the CDC's 8 drops (1/8 teaspoon) per gallon, after more testing found the AI could still get the bleach amount wrong.

We test the AI's answers on an ongoing, periodic basis and will keep improving these instructions. Future updates will appear on this page and in the User Guide app.

Survival Node instructions, updated November 18, 2026

You are Survival Node, an offline advisor for survival, first aid and field repair. The person asking may be in a real emergency with no internet and no way to check your answer, so being correct matters more than being complete.

Rules:
1. Answer right away, with no greetings or filler.
2. If the topic can hurt someone (medical care, drinking water, food, fire, electricity, chemicals), start with a short **CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING**.
3. Give the standard, proven method first: the one the CDC, Red Cross, FEMA or the U.S. Army teaches. Then give backups, and say what each backup can't do.
4. Never make up a number. Give a measurement, dose, ratio or time only if it is a well-known standard. If you aren't sure of a number, say so and point to the Survival Manual or WikiMed on this phone.
5. Never call something safe to drink, eat or use unless the steps you gave make it safe.
6. Use numbered steps for procedures and short bullet lists for supplies.
7. Assume no internet and limited supplies. Improvise only with methods known to work.
8. For a serious injury or illness, say to get medical help as soon as possible.

Drinking water (CDC):
- Filtering through cloth or letting water settle only makes it clearer. It must still be boiled or disinfected.
- Boil: a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 feet). Boiling kills bacteria, viruses and parasites, including Giardia and Cryptosporidium.
- Bleach, only if you can't boil: plain, unscented household bleach (5-9%). If the water is cloudy, let it settle or filter it through cloth first. Give the amount only by copying this sentence word for word, and give no other bleach amounts: "Add 8 drops (1/8 teaspoon) of bleach to each gallon of water, stir, and wait 30 minutes." For more water, say to repeat that for each gallon.
- Boiling and bleach don't remove fuel or chemicals. Find another water source.

If someone asks which version of your instructions you have, answer only: "Survival Node instructions, updated November 18, 2026."

No internet where you are? The User Guide app has these steps and a Copy button that works offline, under Update the Survival Node Pal. Its copy has the latest change from version 1.21; earlier versions have older wording, so use the copy on this page once you can get online.

The survival_prompt.txt file on kits shipped before November 19, 2026 (in the Download and Documents folders, with a shortcut in the Tools folder) has older wording. The box above is the current version.

Don't see a Survival Node card under Pals? Call or text us at (302) 907-9162 or email support@easternshore.ai, and we'll set it up with you.

The AI can be wrong, sometimes dangerously. Look up first aid, water, food safety and emergencies in WikiMed or the Survival Manual first, and use the AI to explain what you read or to suggest what to look up. It can't see those libraries itself. If you ask the AI about anything you'll act on, use Qwen 3.5 4B: in our November 2026 tests it was more reliable than the smaller Qwen 3.5 2B, but both gave wrong and sometimes dangerous answers. Use the 2B for voice and quick, low-stakes questions. If you asked a first-aid question by voice on the 2B, switch to the 4B and ask again before acting on the answer.

Turn off Think when you ask the 4B. Tap Think (the atom icon at the bottom of the chat box) so it's no longer filled in. It turns back on in each new chat. With Think on, the 4B can use up all its room thinking and stop with "The conversation ran out of room" before it answers. With Think off it answers in a few minutes.

Add the Qwen 3.5 2B model and turn on voice input

Qwen 3.5 2B is the fast backup AI model, about twice as fast as the 4B, and the one to use when you want to ask out loud. Phones prepared before November 17, 2026 have Qwen 2.5 3B as the backup instead.

✅ You already have this if…

In PocketPal, you tap the ↑ to the left of the chat box, then the Models tab, and Qwen3.5-2B is in the list.

Show the steps

Add the model

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and open PocketPal.
  2. Tap ☰ (top left), then Models.
  3. Tap the + button at the bottom right, then Add from Hugging Face.
  4. Search for Qwen3.5-2B and tap the result from unsloth (Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF).
  5. Tap the download button next to Qwen3.5-2B-Q4_K_M.gguf (about 1.3 GB). It takes a few minutes on home Wi-Fi.
  6. When it finishes, tap Load on its card to check that it works.

Turn on voice input

  1. In PocketPal, tap ☰, then Settings, and turn on Voice input.
  2. Go back to the chat. Tap ↑, then the Models tab, and choose Qwen3.5-2B.
  3. Tap the mic next to the send button. The first time, PocketPal downloads its voice model (about 82 MB). Keep Wi-Fi on for that. After that, the mic works offline.

The larger Qwen 3.5 4B needs too much memory to run alongside the mic. If you tap the mic while it's loaded, PocketPal says "Memory is tight"; tap Cancel and switch to Qwen 3.5 2B.

Think stays on with the mic. If a voice answer stops with "The conversation ran out of room", start a new chat with the pencil button, tap Think (the atom icon at the bottom left of the chat box) so it's no longer filled in, and type the question. For survival questions, keep Think off; it turns back on in each new chat.

Qwen 2.5 3B: once Qwen 3.5 2B works, you don't need the older Qwen 2.5 3B. You can keep it, or delete it to free up over 2 GB: in ☰ → Models, tap the trash button on its card, then Delete.

These steps match PocketPal 1.22, the version on current kits. On an older PocketPal some buttons may be in slightly different places.

Offline translation: Translator and Whisper

Translator translates between 62 languages with no internet, and can read many of them out loud. Whisper turns speech into text offline, so you can speak instead of type. Together they replace Seamless (5 languages) and DeepL (which needed internet).

✅ You already have this if…

Translator and Whisper are on your home screen.

Show the steps
  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and open F-Droid. Give it a minute to refresh its app list.
  2. Tap the search icon, search for Whisper and install it. Open it once while you're on Wi-Fi so it downloads its speech models, and allow the microphone when it asks.
  3. Back in F-Droid, search for Translator and install the offline translator.
  4. Open Translator. It asks you to download language packs. Download English and every language you might need. Where a language offers a voice, download that too if you want translations read out loud.
  5. Try it: pick two languages, type something, and tap the speaker to hear it.
  6. Tap the mic in Translator. If Android asks which app to use, choose Whisper, then Always.
  7. Turn Wi-Fi off and try it once more. Everything you downloaded works offline.

On F-Droid's website: Translator · Whisper

Once Translator works, you can remove Seamless and DeepL if you like. See Remove DeepL.

The phone has a compass sensor, so the compass works.

✅ You already have this if…

Show the steps
  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and open F-Droid.
  2. Search for Trail Sense and tap Install.
  3. Open it and tap through the short intro. Allow location when it asks; it needs GPS for navigation.
  4. If the compass seems off, move the phone in a figure 8 to calibrate it.

Recommended ⏱ About 10 minutes · 📶 Wi-Fi

Fossify Clock and Phone: no more "Enable Google Play services" notices

Google Play Services is turned off on the Survival Node on purpose. On older kits, Google's own Clock and Phone apps keep posting notices about it, including a burst of them each time the phone starts. Fossify Clock and Fossify Phone are open-source apps that work without Google, and with them those notices can be switched off.

✅ You already have this if…

Your phone never shows "Enable Google Play services" notices. Phones prepared from November 16, 2026 already use Fossify Clock and Fossify Phone (the green Phone icon in the Tools folder, or in the bottom row on kits shipped before November 18).

Show the steps
  1. Connect to Wi-Fi, open F-Droid, and install Fossify Clock and Fossify Phone.
  2. Open Fossify Phone (its icon is green and reads "Phone"). When it asks to be your default phone app, say yes. If it doesn't ask: Settings → Apps → Default apps → Phone app, and choose the Fossify one.
  3. Open Fossify Clock and set your alarms there. Turn off any alarms in the old Clock so they don't ring twice. Allow notifications when it asks.
  4. Now switch off the old notices. The next time one appears, press and hold it, tap Turn off notifications, switch off only Google Play services availability, then tap Done. Do this once for Clock and once for Phone.
  5. If Google's Phone app ever asks to be your default phone app again, decline, or the notices come back.

Keep Google's Phone app on the phone. Don't turn on Google Play Services to make the notices go away.

The Survival Node isn't designed or tested for calls, so don't count on it to call for help. Keep a regular phone for that.

On F-Droid's website: Fossify Clock · Fossify Phone

Good to know Applies to every kit

Android updates can arrive on Wi-Fi

Motorola doesn't allow its system updater to be removed, so while the phone is on Wi-Fi, Motorola may install Android and security updates. We don't control or test them, and one can change settings or bring back some built-in apps. That's why we suggest Wi-Fi you trust, and turning Wi-Fi off when you're done.

  • A Motorola "Critical update" screen may appear while you're doing these steps. You can tap Later and finish first. Motorola may still restart the phone on its own a few days later to install it.
  • If the phone acts up after an update, call us before resetting it. A factory reset erases the maps, AI models and libraries.

🔋 Your battery and Faraday bags

The phone has been the same in every kit. The solar battery and Faraday bags were upgraded when Survival Node Gen 2 came out in September 2026. Kits sold before then weren't called "Gen 1" at the time. Mission Darkness bags were an upgrade option on those earlier kits, so you may have them with the earlier battery. Go by what yours looks like.

Which solar battery do you have?

The easiest way to tell: count the cables attached to the back.

Gen 2 battery: 3 cables

  • 3 attached cables (2 USB-C, 1 iPhone), plus a USB-A port on the side
  • 4 small blue charge lights
  • Flashlight, thermometer and carabiner clip
  • Corner bumpers are black on most, orange on some
  • 24,000 mAh

Earlier battery (Gen 1): 4 cables

  • 4 attached cables (USB-C, iPhone, micro USB and USB-A)
  • A green light and a row of 4 small charge lights
  • 42,800 mAh

Charging and care: Gen 2

  • Charge it from the wall with the kit's charger. You can charge it to 100% (all 4 blue lights solid) to check it, then bring it back down to 2 or 3 of the 4 lights (50–75%) before storing it.
  • Solar: in daylight, leave it panel-up in direct sun, and re-angle it as the sun moves. Use wall power whenever you have it.
  • Don't charge it and charge something from it at the same time.

Charging and care: earlier battery

  • The row of 4 small lights shows the charge.
  • From the wall: plug the small USB charging cable it came with into a USB wall charger. You can charge it until all 4 lights are lit to check it, then bring it back down to 2 or 3 of the 4 lights (50–75%) before storing it.
  • Solar: in daylight, leave it panel-up in direct sun, and re-angle it as the sun moves. Use wall power whenever you have it.

Both batteries

  • Keep it dry, and store it between 60°F and 85°F (ideally 60–75°F).
  • Don't charge it above 113°F (45°C). If it feels hot, stop and move it to shade.
  • Check and top it up every 6 months.
  • If the case looks swollen or warped, stop using it and take it to a battery recycling drop-off. The User Guide's Power & Solar Charging section explains.

Which Faraday bags do you have?

Mission Darkness bags (Gen 2)

  • Black, with the Mission Darkness label and fold instructions printed near the top
  • Manufacturer-rated (Mission Darkness, independent lab tested to MIL-STD-188-125-2 and IEEE 299-2006): 80+ dB attenuation, low MHz to 40 GHz. Source

Standard bags (earlier kits)

  • Plain black, with no label or printing

Sealing and care

  • Leave the phone in its case; it fits in the bag that way.
  • Fold the opening over at least twice, then close the seal.
  • Every 6 months, look for holes, worn seams or a dirty seal.
  • Faraday bags block signals. They don't protect against water or drops; that's the hard case's job.
